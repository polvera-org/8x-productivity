# Twitter/X Content Creator

## When to Use

Activate this skill when:
- Writing new Twitter/X content (posts, threads, articles)
- Optimizing existing content for better engagement
- Crafting attention-grabbing hooks
- Adapting long-form content to social media
- Creating content for business, technical, or creative audiences
- Building authority and generating warm leads through content

## Core Philosophy

**Every word must earn its place.**

Great Twitter/X content combines:
- **Clarity**: Simple words, complex ideas
- **Tension**: Contrarian takes, pattern interrupts, unresolved questions
- **Authority**: Proof over promises, specifics over generalities
- **Value**: Reader-first, save-worthy insights

The goal isn't just engagement—it's making readers *believe* in your perspective and want to share your ideas.

## The Writing Process

### Step 1: Choose Your Format

Ask: What depth does this idea deserve?
- **One insight** → Single post
- **Connected ideas or story** → Thread
- **Framework + implementation** → X Article

### Step 2: Identify the Core Tension

Every piece needs a hook that creates cognitive dissonance:
- What commonly accepted belief is wrong?
- What counterintuitive pattern have you noticed?
- What painful truth are people avoiding?

**Examples:**
- "Most productivity advice makes you less productive"
- "The best marketers don't sell—they diagnose"
- "Your first 10 customers matter more than your first 1,000"

### Step 3: Write the Hook First

The first line determines everything. Spend 50% of your time here.

See `references/hook-techniques.md` for 8 proven hook frameworks.

### Step 4: Build Body Content

- Short paragraphs (1-3 sentences max)
- One idea per chunk
- Heavy whitespace
- Bold for emphasis (sparingly)
- Never use emojis or hashtags (unless specifically requested)

### Step 5: End with Momentum

- Single posts: End on a punchline or quotable insight
- Threads: Memorable takeaway that ties everything together
- Articles: Integration framework that makes action inevitable

## Voice & Style Guidelines

### Universal Principles

| ✅ Do This | ❌ Avoid This |
|------------|---------------|
| Write in 8th-9th grade vocabulary | Jargon without explanation |
| Use conversational contractions | Corporate or academic tone |
| First-person authority when sharing experience | Generic "you should" advice |
| Direct second-person address | Passive constructions |
| Declarative statements with few hedges | "I think," "maybe," "sort of" |
| Specifics as proof | Vague promises |

### Tonal Checkpoints

Before publishing, verify:
- [ ] Would a smart builder share this?
- [ ] Is every line quotable?
- [ ] Does it sound like spoken wisdom, not written content?
- [ ] Would someone screenshot this to save it?

## Format-Specific Workflows

### For Single Posts

**Structure:**
1. Hook (contrarian or insight-driven)
2. Body (1-2 short paragraphs, concrete proof)
3. Optional CTA (only if natural)

**Length:** Under 280 characters (Twitter) or concise for X

**Example:**
> "Most productivity advice makes you less productive.
>
> The real hack? Do one thing until it's done. 
> Multitasking is just rapid task-switching with extra anxiety."

See full guide: `references/post-writing-guide.md`

### For Threads

**Structure:**
- Max 8 tweets
- Each tweet = one complete idea or proof point
- Start with tension-based hook (no "thread below" phrasing)
- Build logically or narratively
- End with memorable takeaway

**Flow:**
1. Hook (the tension)
2. Context (why this matters)
3-6. Body (evidence, examples, framework)
7. Synthesis (the insight)
8. Takeaway (what to do with it)

See full guide: `references/thread-writing-guide.md`

### For X Articles

**Structure (Dan Koe 170M view format):**
1. Clickbait-utility headline
2. Contrarian opening hook
3. Problem diagnosis (sections I-V)
4. Protocol/solution (section VI)
5. Integration framework (section VII)

**Formatting:**
- Roman numeral sections (I, II, III...)
- ~4,000 words with heavy whitespace
- Progressive reveal: philosophy → framework → protocol
- Borrowed authority (quotes from recognized experts)
- Specific protocol with granular timestamps

See full guide: `references/article-writing-guide.md`

## Adaptation Guidelines

### From Video to Article

When converting video transcripts:
1. Extract the core thesis and supporting points
2. Remove verbal filler and transitions
3. Add structural signposts (headers, section breaks)
4. Expand on implicit ideas the speaker assumed audience knew
5. End with actionable integration steps

### From Long-Form to Thread

1. Identify 5-7 key ideas
2. Write each as standalone but connected tweets
3. Ensure each tweet provides value independently
4. Create narrative flow between tweets
5. First tweet must work without context

### From Article to Single Post

1. Find the single most quotable insight
2. Strip to core tension + proof
3. Add punchline or question

## Quality Checklist

Before delivering content, verify:

### Hook Quality
- [ ] Creates immediate curiosity or tension
- [ ] Promises specific value
- [ ] Doesn't rely on clickbait without substance

### Body Quality
- [ ] Every sentence advances the idea
- [ ] No filler words or phrases
- [ ] Concrete examples over abstract concepts
- [ ] Proper pacing (whitespace, short paragraphs)

### Technical Quality
- [ ] No spelling/grammar errors
- [ ] Proper formatting for platform
- [ ] Character count within limits (if applicable)

### Engagement Quality
- [ ] Would you share this?
- [ ] Is there a quotable line?
- [ ] Does it create belief shift or new perspective?

## Example Workflow

**User:** "Help me write a thread about why most AI tools fail"

**Agent:**
1. Identify format: Thread (3-8 tweets) for moderate depth
2. Find tension: "Most AI tools fail because they're built backwards"
3. Select hook type: PAS (Problem → Agitation → Solution)
4. Draft structure:
   - Hook: The backwards-building problem
   - Context: Why feature-first fails
   - Proof: Examples of failed vs. successful approaches
   - Framework: How to build AI tools that work
   - Takeaway: The mindset shift needed
5. Write with tight, quotable language
6. Deliver with formatting optimized for Twitter/X

## Remember

**Your job isn't to explain—it's to make readers believe.**

Every piece of content should feel like:
- A secret someone needed to hear
- An insight that changes how they see something
- A perspective worth stealing to sound smart

When in doubt: cut words, add tension, make it quotable.
